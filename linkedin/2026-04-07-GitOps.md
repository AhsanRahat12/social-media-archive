# Post 02 — GitOps (LinkedIn)

---

I deleted a resource from my cluster and Flux put it right back.

That was the moment GitOps actually clicked for me.

Here is what changed in how I think about infrastructure:

Before GitOps, everything was manual. I applied manifests one by one with kubectl, tweaked things directly in the cluster, and had no reliable record of what was actually running or why.

After GitOps, my Git repo is my cluster. Flux runs a constant reconciliation loop, checks what is in Git, and makes sure the cluster matches it exactly. Always.

The implications of that are huge.

✅ Delete something by accident, Flux restores it.
✅ Merge a bad change, `git revert` is your rollback.
✅ Want to know what changed and when, check the Git log.
✅ Switch to a new cluster, point Flux at the same repo and it rebuilds everything.

The config lives in Git, not in the cluster.

That distinction sounds small. It is not.
