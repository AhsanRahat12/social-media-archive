Every device in your home has its own IP address. Your internet provider gives you exactly one public IP. So how are dozens of devices online at the same time?

NAT. And most people have no idea it is happening.

Private IPs like 192.168.x.x cannot communicate directly over the internet. Your router translates between the two worlds. The version it uses is called PAT, and the trick is ports.

When three devices all hit the internet at once:

1. Phone sends a request. Router swaps the private IP for the public one. Assigns port 60001.
2. Laptop does the same. Same public IP, port 60002.
3. TV does the same. Same public IP, port 60003.

From the outside, all three look identical. Same public IP. But the router tracks every mapping. Response comes back on port 60001? Straight to your phone.

✅ One public IP shared across every device.
✅ Every connection tracked individually via unique ports.
✅ The internet sees one address. Your router handles the rest.

This is also why devices behind NAT cannot easily receive unsolicited inbound connections. No outbound request means no mapping in the table means no way in.

Did you know this was happening every time you went online? 👇
