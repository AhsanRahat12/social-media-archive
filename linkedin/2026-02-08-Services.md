# LinkedIn Post: Kubernetes Services

---

I struggled with Kubernetes Services until I understood this: they're not about networking complexity, they're about solving a simple problem.

Pods are ephemeral. They get deleted, recreated, and moved across nodes constantly. Their IP addresses change every time. How do you build reliable applications on top of something so unstable?

That's where Services come in.

**The Problem Services Solve**

Imagine you have a frontend deployment with 10 replicas. When you update your app, old pods get thrown away and new ones are created. Their IP addresses change. Manually tracking these IP changes is impossible at scale.

Services provide a consistent address to access your pods, no matter how many times they change.

**Think of Services Like a Phone Receptionist**

You call one number (the Service). The receptionist routes you to an available employee (Pod). Employees come and go, but the main number stays the same. Whether that number is internal only or public facing depends on the Service type.

**Three Service Types You Need to Know**

✅ ClusterIP: Internal only, accessible within the cluster. Default type with a stable internal IP.

✅ NodePort: Exposes a port on each node. Allows direct access through any node's IP address. Avoid when possible.

✅ LoadBalancer: Creates a cloud load balancer with an external IP. Used for production external access.

**The Real Power**

When your application points at a Service, you can do rolling updates seamlessly. Remove one pod, create a new one. The Service handles routing requests to available pods. You don't need to know which pod is old or new. Kubernetes handles it.

This is how you build resilient, scalable applications on Kubernetes.
