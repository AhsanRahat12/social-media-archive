I'm about to add health checks to a few services in my home lab. Before I added them, I wanted to actually understand them properly, not just copy-paste a probe block and hope.

Turns out liveness and readiness probes sound like the same thing and are not, and mixing them up is one of the easiest mistakes to make in Kubernetes.

A liveness probe answers one question: is this process still alive, or should Kubernetes restart it? Fail it enough times and the pod gets killed and replaced.

A readiness probe answers a completely different question: is this pod actually able to serve traffic right now? Fail it and the pod stays alive, Kubernetes just stops routing requests to it until it passes again.

The part that took me a minute to actually get: a process can be technically running, liveness passes, while being completely unable to do its job. Still booting. Waiting on a database connection. Mid-migration. If you only configure a liveness probe and skip readiness, that half-broken pod keeps receiving live traffic the entire time, because nothing ever told Kubernetes not to send requests to it.

Short version, now that it's clicked: liveness decides whether to restart. Readiness decides whether to route traffic. Two different switches, and using one to do the other's job is how "healthy" pods end up serving errors.

Now I actually know what I'm writing before I write it. Have you ever chased a bug that turned out to just be a missing readiness probe?
