Have you ever wondered how the internet knows how to get data from one side of the world to the other?

It's not magic. It's BGP, Border Gateway Protocol, and it's the protocol that holds the entire internet together.

Here's the simplest way to think about it. The internet is made up of thousands of independent networks, called autonomous systems. Your ISP is one. Google is one. Amazon is one. Each one manages its own chunk of IP addresses.

BGP is how these networks talk to each other. It's essentially a routing protocol where each network announces to its neighbours, "hey, I can reach these IP addresses, and here's how to get to me."

When you load a website, BGP has already figured out the best path across dozens of these networks to get your data there and back.

A few things that make BGP fascinating:

1. It's based on trust. Networks announce routes and others accept them, mostly without verification.
2. A single misconfigured BGP announcement can accidentally reroute huge chunks of internet traffic. This has happened before.
3. BGP wasn't designed with security in mind. It was built for a smaller, more trusted internet.

It's one of those protocols that quietly runs everything, and most people have no idea it exists.
