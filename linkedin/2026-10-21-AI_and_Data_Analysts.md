When ChatGPT launched in late 2022, headlines said data analysts would be obsolete.

Four years later, I’m still here.

What changed is what I spend my time on. As an analyst, I use Claude regularly in my day to day work, the repetitive, regular parts of the job get automated away.

What doesn’t get automated is judgment, and taste. Knowing whether a number actually makes sense for the business, catching a mismatch that looks fine on paper but isn’t, applying domain knowledge AI doesn’t have. That part is still on me.

It’s also let me venture into things I wouldn’t have touched before, implement them faster, and actually learn from doing it instead of just reading about it. That space to experiment has made the work more creative, not less.

And despite everything changing around it, Excel is still the one tool I open every single day. Old reliable hasn’t gone anywhere, it’s just not doing the boring parts alone anymore.

For analysts, the move isn’t resisting AI or handing it the keys. It’s automating with supervision and leaning further into the business side of the work.
