TCP and UDP both live at Layer 4. Both transport data. But they make completely opposite bets about what matters most.

TCP bets on reliability. UDP bets on speed. And the use case determines which bet wins.

Here is what you are actually choosing between:

TCP does a lot of work behind the scenes. It establishes a connection before sending anything. It numbers every segment so they can be reordered if they arrive out of sequence. It waits for acknowledgements and retransmits anything that gets lost. It controls how fast data flows so the receiver does not get overwhelmed.

All of that takes time. But when you are sending an email, transferring a file, or loading a webpage, losing data is not acceptable. A missing packet means corrupted data. TCP is the right bet.

UDP does none of that. No handshake, no sequencing, no acknowledgements, no retransmission. It wraps your data and fires it at the destination. If packets get lost, they are gone. If they arrive out of order, they stay out of order.

That sounds like a weakness. For some use cases it is exactly what you want:

✅ Video streaming. A dropped frame is better than buffering. A slightly degraded picture beats waiting.
✅ Online gaming. A slightly wrong position is better than a delayed one. Latency kills the experience, not the occasional lost packet.
✅ DNS. A quick question and answer. No need for a full connection setup just to resolve a domain.
✅ VoIP. A tiny audio glitch is far better than a noticeable delay in conversation.

The choice is never TCP good, UDP bad. It is always: what does this use case actually need?
