Every network problem you'll ever debug traces back to one of 7 layers. Most engineers skip learning them. Then spend hours troubleshooting in the wrong place.
The OSI model is not just theory. It is a structured way to think about where problems actually live:

- Physical: is the cable plugged in? Is the signal even reaching?
- Data Link: are frames being delivered within the local network?
- Network: is the packet being routed to the right destination?
- Transport: is TCP completing the handshake? Is the port open?
- Session: is the connection being tracked and maintained?
- Presentation: is the data being encrypted or formatted correctly?
- Application: is the actual service responding as expected?

When something breaks, engineers who know this model start at Layer 1 and work up. Engineers who don't? They restart the server and hope for the best.

Learning the OSI model did not just teach me networking. It taught me how to think about problems systematically, which is arguably more valuable.
