Most people in DevOps and cloud never have to think about fibre. But here is why they should.

Not all fibre is the same, and choosing the wrong type for your setup can be an expensive mistake.

There are two types, single-mode and multi-mode, and each one does a completely different job.

Multi-mode fibre has a wider core, which means light bounces around inside it as it travels. This works great over short distances, think inside a data centre or between buildings on the same campus. It is cheaper and easier to work with, but that bouncing light degrades over longer runs.

Single-mode fibre has a much narrower core. Light travels in a straight line, which means it can go much further without losing signal quality. It is used for long distance runs, between cities, across countries, under oceans. It costs more, but when distance matters, it is the only option.

A simple way to remember it:

1. Multi-mode: short distances, lower cost, great for inside buildings.
2. Single-mode: long distances, higher cost, built for the backbone.

Understanding this helps explain why some infrastructure decisions are made the way they are, even when you never touch the cables yourself.
