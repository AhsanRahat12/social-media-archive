## Tweet 1

I struggled with Kubernetes Services until I understood this:

They're not about networking complexity. They're about solving one simple problem.

Pods are ephemeral. They die and get recreated constantly. Their IP addresses change every time.

Here's what Services actually do: 🧵

---

## Tweet 2

Services provide a consistent address to access your pods, no matter how many times they change.

Think of Services like a phone receptionist:
• You call one number (the Service)
• The receptionist routes you to an available employee (Pod)
• Employees come and go, but the main number stays the same

---

## Tweet 3

Three Service types you need to know:

1. ClusterIP: Internal only, accessible within the cluster. Default type with stable internal IP.

2. NodePort: Exposes a port on each node. Allows direct access through node IPs. Avoid when possible.

3. LoadBalancer: Creates a cloud load balancer with external IP.

---

## Tweet 4

The real power of Services:

When your app points at a Service, you can do rolling updates seamlessly. Remove one pod, create a new one. The Service handles routing to available pods.

You don't track which pod is old or new. Kubernetes handles it.
