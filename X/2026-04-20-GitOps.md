# Post — Watching Flux Reconcile (X / Twitter)

---

**Tweet 1**
The first time I pushed to GitHub after implementing GitOps, I just sat there watching my terminal.

I was not sure it was going to work.

Then it did 🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
The cluster updated itself.

No kubectl apply. No manual anything.

Flux just picked it up and made it happen.

---

**Tweet 3**
Here is what happened under the hood:

1. Pushed an updated manifest to GitHub.
2. Flux detected the change within seconds.
3. Compared Git to what was running in the cluster.
4. New pod spun up. Old one terminated cleanly.

I did not touch the cluster once.

---

**Tweet 4**
That was the moment I understood why teams and enterprises adopt GitOps as an industry standard.

The cluster can only ever be in a state that exists in Git.

No undocumented changes. No mystery configs.

---

**Tweet 5**
Every change has a commit.
Every commit has a history.
Every history has a reason.

That is the discipline GitOps enforces.

---

**Tweet 6**
Once you work this way it is very hard to go back to doing things manually.
