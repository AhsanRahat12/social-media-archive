# From Soil Samples to CI/CD Pipelines: Navigating a Career Switch into DevOps in the Age of AI

There's a moment in every career switcher's journey where you ask yourself: *"How did I end up here?"*

For me, the answer starts in a lab analyzing soil samples, passes through spreadsheets and SQL queries, and ends — for now — in a terminal window, debugging a Kubernetes cluster at midnight.

---

## The Unlikely Foundation

I graduated with a BSc in Environmental Science. On the surface, that has nothing to do with DevOps. But dig a little deeper and the connections are everywhere.

Environmental Science pulled me through chemistry, geology, biology, and statistics — often all in the same week. You learn to think in systems. You learn that everything is connected, that a change in one variable ripples through everything else.

Somewhere along the way I discovered GIS and data pipelines, and fell in love with programming — not just the output, but the *process* of building something that takes raw, messy input and transforms it into something meaningful.

That feeling never left me.

---

## The Data Analyst Chapter

After graduating I landed a data analyst role at Green World. Day to day the work lives in Excel and databases — querying data, building reports, keeping things accurate. Unglamorous a lot of the time. But it planted a question that wouldn't go away.

*Where is this database actually running? How does it connect to everything else? When something breaks, what's actually broken?*

I kept bumping into the edges of my own understanding — and instead of frustration, I found it fascinating. The data was just the surface. I wanted to understand the infrastructure underneath. I wanted to know how the pieces connected, how systems talked to each other, how the whole thing stayed running.

That curiosity didn't have a name for a while. Then I found DevOps — and everything clicked.

---

## Where I Am Now

I'm still at Green World by day — a job I genuinely enjoy — while building DevOps projects on the side and taking part in the **KubeCraft internship program**, getting hands-on with Kubernetes in a real, structured environment.

The breadth of DevOps can be overwhelming. Containers, orchestration, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, cloud platforms, monitoring, security — there's always more to learn. But that's also what keeps me showing up.

---

## On Using AI to Learn

I use **Claude Code** regularly — but deliberately. I lean on it to understand how frameworks fit together and how concepts connect. What I don't use it for is debugging and problem-solving. That I keep for myself.

Debugging is where real understanding is built. When something breaks and you have to sit with it — trace the logs, question your assumptions, test your theories — that's when knowledge becomes structural. I want sharp instincts, not shortcuts.

---

## What I'd Say to Anyone Considering the Switch

Your background is not a disadvantage. If you're coming from data work — even Excel and SQL — you're closer than you think. The questions you're already asking about your databases are DevOps questions. Just follow them deeper.

The fundamentals still matter more than ever. AI can generate the code. You still need to understand what it's doing.
